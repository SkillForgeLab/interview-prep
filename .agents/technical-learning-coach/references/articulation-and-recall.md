# Articulation and Recall

Load this file when the learner is ready to practice explanation, recognition, interviews, design reviews, or long-term recall.

## Active recall

Do not rely on rereading alone.

Periodically ask the learner to retrieve earlier ideas without showing the answer first.

Useful recall prompts include:

- "What problem were we trying to remove?"
- "What responsibility moved, and where?"
- "What would make this abstraction unnecessary?"
- "What changes if the decision must happen at runtime?"

Space recall across the conversation rather than testing immediately after every explanation.

## Recognition practice

Give unfamiliar examples and ask the learner to identify:

1. the design pressure,
2. the responsibility that is causing it,
3. whether the learned concept applies,
4. what simpler alternative may be enough.

Vary names and domains so recognition does not depend on memorized class names.

## Articulation progression

Ask the learner to explain the concept first in simple language.

Then refine it through three levels:

```text
Clear personal explanation
          ↓
Precise technical explanation
          ↓
Polished professional explanation
```

Preserve the learner's intended meaning while improving wording.

## Feedback order

When reviewing the learner's explanation, respond in this order:

1. Correct the mental model.
2. Identify missing technical ideas.
3. Improve structure and precision.
4. Improve English wording.
5. Provide a polished version only after explaining the important correction.

Do not hide a conceptual error by merely rewriting the sentence elegantly.

## Professional contexts

Rotate articulation practice across realistic settings:

- interview answer,
- design review,
- code review,
- architecture discussion,
- explaining to another engineer,
- explaining to a less experienced engineer.

Adjust terminology and depth to the audience.

## Concision practice

Once the learner understands a concept, practice multiple explanation lengths:

- one sentence,
- thirty-second explanation,
- two-minute explanation,
- deeper design discussion.

The shorter version must preserve the core causal relationship, not just the definition.
