# Operating Reliable Enterprise Integrations

**Study guide for senior/principal Enterprise and Integration Solution Architect interviews**  
**Scope:** Vendor-neutral reliability design and day-two operations. Detailed Outbox and Saga mechanics belong in the distributed transactions guide.

## 1. Start with a business promise and a failure boundary

An integration is reliable when it completes business work despite slow systems, partial failures, duplicates, and operator intervention. “The message was sent” is a transport observation; “the order is in the ERP and eligible for fulfillment” is a business outcome. Name both and their accountable owners.

**Illustrative assumptions for this guide, not facts about a real employer:** An Order service records a customer request and hands an order to an ERP adapter. The ERP owns credit validation and booking; a separate fulfillment system acts only after ERP confirmation. An order can be *received* without being *accepted for fulfillment*. Normal target: 99% of received orders reach a terminal ERP decision within 15 minutes. During a declared ERP outage, the business permits a clearly labeled pending state and sends customer updates every two hours. Example queue retention is 18 hours. These targets require business agreement and load testing before adoption.

At the design review, ask which actions may proceed without fresh ERP data, which effects are irreversible, how long customers can wait, and who owns each system.

## 2. Bound the failure before it spreads

| Control | Design decision | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Timeouts** | Set a total caller deadline, then smaller per-hop connection and operation timeouts with room for cleanup. Measure downstream tail latency and distinguish connection setup from request processing. | A long hung ERP call consumes workers and connections until unrelated flows fail. A timeout leaves the remote outcome *unknown*. |
| **Bounded retries** | Retry only classified transient failures within a shared attempt and elapsed-time budget. Use exponential backoff with jitter, respect useful `Retry-After` guidance, and preserve the same business operation ID. Check outcome before retrying an ambiguous side effect. | Multiple layers each retrying three times can amplify one request into many ERP calls. Do not retry validation errors or an open breaker. |
| **Circuit breaker** | Open after a measured pattern of ERP failures; fail or defer quickly. Half-open with a small, rate-limited probe set. Scope the breaker to the failing dependency or operation. | A global breaker for all ERP functions can block healthy reads. A breaker without a durable handoff can drop work. |
| **Bulkhead** | Give ERP writes a bounded worker/connections pool separate from order intake, status queries, and other downstreams. Reserve capacity for recovery and operator queries. | One slow dependency exhausts every shared thread or connection. |
| **Throttle and backpressure** | Limit admissions, in-flight calls, and outbound rate at the actual bottleneck. When backlog age or capacity approaches a boundary, slow or reject new submissions with an honest status. | An unlimited producer outruns a finite queue; scaling consumers can overload the recovering ERP. |
| **Buffer** | Use durable storage with measured size, retention, and publisher confirmation. Track the age and business deadline of each item. | A queue smooths a temporary mismatch; it cannot make an indefinitely slower consumer catch up. |

AWS's Builders' Library explains why timeouts, retries, backoff, and jitter are paired, and why a timed-out side effect can already have happened. Microsoft's pattern guidance describes circuit breakers, bulkhead isolation, and throttling as distinct controls. [AWS Builders' Library](https://builder.aws.com/content/3EumjoZascWd1oZiEgL8ORlv3qE/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter), [Circuit breaker](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/circuit-breaker), [Bulkhead](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/bulkhead), [Throttling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/throttling).

**Retry placement matters.** An interactive request may fit one short retry; return “pending” only if work was durably recorded. A background worker can retry longer, then route exhausted work to investigation. Use one owner for the main retry policy; align library settings. Illustrative policy: two status-read attempts within three seconds; ERP writes get up to five attempts over ten minutes with capped exponential delay and full jitter. Neither applies to malformed data or a prolonged outage. Microsoft documents retry-storm risk and finite retry budgets. [Azure transient fault guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/best-practices/transient-faults), [Retry storm antipattern](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/antipatterns/retry-storm/).

**A queue is a buffer, not a recovery plan.** It does not decide whether the ERP already booked an order, whether a later cancellation makes work stale, whether a poison message should block a lane, or whether a replay duplicates a financial effect. Recovery also needs stable operation IDs, outcome lookup, explicit expiry rules, DLQ ownership, guarded replay, capacity headroom, and source-to-destination reconciliation. Queue-based load leveling guidance explicitly calls for durable messages, controlled consumer rate, idempotent processing, DLQ operations, and monitoring when arrivals exceed completions. [Azure queue-based load leveling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/patterns/queue-based-load-leveling).

## 3. Six-hour ERP outage drill

**Scenario assumptions:** ERP write and confirmation APIs are unavailable for six hours. Order intake can durably record a request and show a truthful pending state without fresh ERP checks; it cannot promise credit approval, inventory allocation, shipping, or an invoice. The sustained incoming rate during the outage is **150 orders/minute**, including bursts. Each queued command and metadata average **4 KiB**. After recovery, tested safe ERP capacity is **300 order decisions/minute** while new traffic remains at 150/minute. These are exercise inputs, not recommended production values.

**What continues:** Authenticated requests can be validated against rules that do not require ERP, assigned a stable order ID, and durably recorded as `RECEIVED_PENDING_ERP`. Customers and support can query that state. Independent functions such as catalog browsing or non-ERP notifications continue in separate capacity pools. If the business cannot accept an order without a live credit or price decision, intake instead pauses or responds “unavailable”; a queue must not silently convert a validation requirement into a promise.

**What pauses:** ERP booking, credit-dependent acceptance, inventory release, fulfillment dispatch, invoice generation, and any irreversible charge or shipment linked to ERP approval. Changes such as cancellation are recorded as new intent and checked against current order state before queued booking is sent. A pending order can be withdrawn under a defined rule; the worker must not process an older booking command after cancellation.

**Backlog method:** Estimate `outage arrivals − completions`, using the *highest sustained* rate for the relevant window rather than a daily average. Here, `150 × 60 × 6 = 54,000` commands. Raw payload is about `54,000 × 4 KiB ≈ 211 MiB`. An illustrative **3×** planning factor for broker metadata, copies, and indexing plus **2×** safety headroom gives about **1.3 GiB**; measure the selected broker's actual accounting, quotas, retention, and failover behavior. Count any independent subscription copies and DLQ storage separately. The queue must hold work for the six-hour outage **plus** catch-up and investigation. Spare recovery rate is `300 − 150 = 150/minute`, so ideal catch-up is another **six hours**, before retries and throttling. An 18-hour example retention leaves only six hours of contingency. If safe ERP capacity is at or below new arrivals, the backlog will not shrink; stop or reduce intake, negotiate ERP capacity, or extend the business deadline. Do not claim that more workers create ERP capacity.

**During the outage:** The incident commander declares severity based on customer impact and age of pending work. The adapter opens its ERP breaker, stops repeated probes except limited health checks, and preserves commands durably. On-call staff verify publisher confirmations, queue growth against forecast, oldest age, retention margin, DLQ inflow, and the count of orders still pending. Product and support publish the precise promise: “request received; ERP confirmation pending,” with affected customer segments and next update time. If the projected storage, retention, or business deadline will be crossed, apply pre-agreed intake limits before data loss or misleading acceptance occurs.

**Recovery sequence:** (1) ERP owner confirms service health, quota, and data consistency. (2) Compare pending order IDs with ERP records to find commands that may have succeeded just before the outage; resolve ambiguous outcomes by lookup, not blind resend. (3) Quarantine invalid and expired commands; evaluate cancellations and changed prices or credit conditions under current business policy. (4) Canary a small batch with stable operation IDs. (5) Ramp workers gradually under ERP rate and concurrency limits, watching ERP latency and errors; pause ramp on regression. (6) Process eligible backlog in business priority order if policy allows, recording any order changes from normal sequence. (7) Reconcile accepted, rejected, cancelled, and still-pending counts and amounts against the Order service, ERP, and fulfillment. (8) Close only after discrepancies have owners and customer statuses are accurate. An outage ending does not mean recovery is complete.

**Customer and business impact:** Report received, confirmed, pending, at-risk, and cancelled orders, plus held financial and fulfillment actions. A six-hour outage breaches the illustrative 15-minute target; do not redefine `RECEIVED` as `CONFIRMED`. Finance reviews booked amounts, duplicate invoices, and unsupported fulfillment. Support needs per-order and bulk incident views.

## 4. End-to-end order status that support can trust

Model a **business state** and a **processing state** separately. An order may be `RECEIVED_PENDING_ERP` while its delivery is `QUEUED`, `RETRY_SCHEDULED`, or `QUARANTINED`. Do not expose a queue acknowledgement as customer success. Every transition stores actor/system, timestamp, reason code, prior and new state, source event or command ID, and downstream reference when known. The Order service owns the customer-facing projection; ERP owns its own booking outcome; integration operations owns transport recovery.

**Partial success is a first-class state:** ERP confirmation can succeed while fulfillment release or invoicing fails. Preserve the confirmed ERP fact, mark the incomplete step and its owner, and resume only that step after checking its outcome. Do not restart the whole order or tell the customer it is complete.

| Business state | Meaning shown to support | Expected next evidence; stuck or inconsistent test |
|---|---|---|
| `RECEIVED_PENDING_ERP` | Request recorded; acceptance remains undecided. | Durable command exists. Alert if its age exceeds the normal decision target or no command/exception record exists. |
| `ERP_SUBMITTED` | Adapter attempted booking; outcome may still be unknown. | ERP booking ID or explicit rejection. A timeout requires outcome lookup; repeated submissions with different operation IDs are suspicious. |
| `ERP_CONFIRMED` | ERP accepted and returned its reference. | Matching ERP record, amount, customer, and version; fulfillment release may follow. Missing ERP record is a high-severity discrepancy. |
| `ERP_REJECTED` | ERP made a terminal business rejection. | Reason and customer-safe explanation. It must not be retried as a transient fault. |
| `FULFILLMENT_RELEASED` | Confirmed order handed to fulfillment. | Fulfillment acknowledgement and later completion; no release before ERP confirmation. |
| `COMPLETED` | Agreed business completion criteria met. | Required ERP and fulfillment evidence present. “Message consumed” is insufficient. |
| `CANCELLED` / `EXPIRED` | Order ended by approved rule. | No later ERP booking or fulfillment release unless an exception is recorded and resolved. |
| `RECONCILE_REQUIRED` | Records disagree or an effect is ambiguous. | Assigned owner, discrepancy type, next action, and customer update. Do not guess a success state. |

Give support a single timeline searchable by **order ID**, client reference, ERP booking ID, and incident ID. Show current business state, processing state, last successful hop, last attempted hop, time since last progress, next retry or review time, and a safe customer message. Keep the durable **business ID** distinct from a message ID, an idempotency/operation ID, a correlation ID, and a trace ID. Carry trace context through API calls and messages; link asynchronous spans when a new trace starts. Use correlation IDs to search logs across systems, while the order ID remains the durable join key for support and reconciliation. OpenTelemetry specifies context propagation and trace/log correlation; its messaging conventions identify message-level telemetry. [OpenTelemetry context propagation](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/concepts/context-propagation/), [OpenTelemetry logging specification](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/otel/logs/), [OpenTelemetry messaging spans](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/specs/semconv/messaging/messaging-spans/).

An **audit trail** records who changed business state or authorized replay, the payload version, and before/after values. Protect it from ordinary edits; redact secrets and unnecessary personal data. Traces diagnose timing, but sampling and retention make them unsuitable as the sole business ledger.

## 5. Quarantine, stale work, replay, and reconciliation

Classify failures before acting. **Transient**: timeout, temporary unavailability, or rate limit; retry within budget. **Permanent data or contract fault**: invalid schema, missing required field, or rejected business rule; quarantine with reason and owner. **Ambiguous side effect**: ERP may have committed before the adapter timed out; query by stable operation ID or business key, then decide. **Stale work**: deadline expired, order cancelled, version superseded, or the business precondition changed; discard or compensate only under an approved domain rule. A DLQ contains evidence of unresolved work; it is not a success bucket. Product documentation for one broker illustrates that dead-lettered records require explicit inspection and disposition, and may remain until removed. [Azure Service Bus DLQ documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/service-bus-messaging/service-bus-dead-letter-queues).

**Controlled replay contract:** Select a bounded set by incident, order ID, error reason, and time range. Export a manifest with item IDs, original operation IDs, payload/schema versions, intended destination, current business state, expected effects, and approvals. Dry-run eligibility and outcome lookup first. Replay through an isolated, rate-limited lane; preserve IDs and link new attempt IDs to originals. Canary, compare results, and expand in batches. Stop on unexpected duplicate suppression, latency, failure, or mismatch. Record each decision and operator. Never “redrive all” from a DLQ into the live queue without checking cancellations, TTL, schema, and already-completed effects.

**Reconciliation contract:** Define the authoritative record for each fact. Compare received orders with ERP decisions by stable key; compare ERP-confirmed orders with fulfillment and invoices by reference, amount, quantity, and version. Match by business key, not only daily totals. Classify missing, duplicate, divergent, and late records; allow a documented settlement window; assign every residual discrepancy. A duplicate message without a duplicate booking is transport noise; a duplicate invoice is a business incident. Reconcile after outages and periodically during normal operations to catch quiet omissions.

## 6. Operational runbook and ownership

1. **Alert and declare.** Page the integration on-call for breached customer decision time, rapidly rising oldest age, or critical mismatch. Open an incident record with scope, first bad time, affected orders, and incident commander. The ERP owner confirms downstream health; product/support owns customer messaging.
2. **Triage and contain.** Check intake, broker publish confirmations, queue age/depth, adapter failures, ERP latency, breaker state, and recent changes. Set bounded outbound rate or pause a bad lane. Preserve evidence and avoid multiple teams changing retry settings simultaneously.
3. **Quarantine.** Isolate poison or stale items with reason, original identifiers, and owner. Verify healthy work continues if ordering rules permit. Escalate financial or fulfillment ambiguity immediately.
4. **Replay.** Approve manifest and eligibility policy, look up unknown outcomes, canary, ramp under quotas, and log each batch. Have a stop condition and rollback/compensation owner.
5. **Reconcile.** Compare per-order states and totals across source, ERP, fulfillment, and finance. Resolve or explicitly assign residual discrepancies; verify customer-facing statuses.
6. **Escalate and communicate.** Incident commander coordinates; operations lead changes production; communications lead updates support, customers, and leadership at agreed intervals. Escalate to ERP/vendor and business decision owners when capacity, retention, or promise deadlines are at risk.
7. **Close and learn.** Close after backlog is within normal age, no unknown-effect work remains unowned, discrepancy targets are met, and support has an accurate resolution statement. Record timeline, customer impact, replay manifest, and prevention actions.

Clear command, operations, and communications roles reduce conflicting changes during an incident; Google's SRE incident response guidance describes that role split. [Google SRE incident response](https://sre.google/workbook/incident-response/).

## 7. Metrics and alerts that connect machinery to outcomes

**Illustrative alert thresholds below assume** a 15-minute normal ERP-decision target, 18-hour queue retention, and the exercise workload. Calibrate from real distributions, error budgets, tenant tiers, and observed recovery capacity. Page on customer harm or imminent data loss; send lesser trends to a work queue. Google SRE recommends latency, traffic, errors, and saturation, including tail latency and correctness beyond HTTP codes. [Google SRE monitoring](https://sre.google/sre-book/monitoring-distributed-systems/).

| Signal | Why it matters | Example alert and first question |
|---|---|---|
| **Queue depth** by route and tenant | Reveals accumulated units and capacity pressure, but not how long customers wait. | Warn if projected fill time is under four hours; are arrivals greater than completions? |
| **Consumer lag** by subscription/partition | Reveals unread position and a stalled or hot lane hidden by aggregate counts. | Warn on sustained growth for 10 minutes; which group or key is falling behind? |
| **Age of oldest eligible work** and age bands | Directly measures waiting time and retention exposure. Exclude intentionally deferred items only with explicit classification. | Page at 15 minutes outside a declared exception; how long until the first item expires? |
| **Failure, retry, and DLQ rates** by reason | Separates transient dependency trouble from poison data and retry amplification. | Page on sustained ERP failure plus rising age; is this one bad record or a broad outage? |
| **Latency** p50/p95/p99 for intake, ERP call, and end-to-end decision | Tail latency consumes deadline and worker capacity; a fast `500` is still failure. | Warn when p99 approaches its budget; where is time spent? |
| **Throughput** arrivals, completions, and safe ERP capacity | Determines whether the backlog can clear and estimates catch-up time. | Warn if completion rate remains at or below arrival rate after recovery; is the ERP the bottleneck? |
| **Business discrepancies**: missing/duplicate/divergent orders, invoices, releases | Detects silent wrong outcomes that healthy queues and HTTP success rates miss. | Page immediately for an unauthorized fulfillment or duplicate invoice; which source owns the fact? |

Also watch publish-confirm failures, breaker state, throttled admissions, in-flight calls, storage/retention margin, replay volume, and orders in `RECONCILE_REQUIRED`. Dashboard by business cohort and dependency; avoid high-cardinality order IDs as metric labels. Use logs or a status index for per-order lookup.

## 8. Five interviewer challenges

| Challenge | Strong answer | Anti-pattern |
|---|---|---|
| “Why not retry until the ERP returns?” | Classify the error, cap attempts and elapsed time, use jitter, respect downstream capacity, and preserve a stable operation ID. During a long outage, defer durably and expose pending status. | Infinite immediate retry, or retries at every layer. |
| “The ERP call timed out. Did the order fail?” | No. The outcome is unknown. Query ERP by operation/business key; then record confirmation, rejection, or unresolved ambiguity before any resend. | Treat timeout as proof of no effect and create a second order. |
| “We added a queue. Are we resilient now?” | Only if retention and capacity cover outage plus catch-up, consumers have bounded rate, stale/poison work has disposition, replay is controlled, and reconciliation verifies effects. | Queue depth as the only health signal; automatic bulk redrive. |
| “Can we keep taking orders during six hours of ERP downtime?” | Only under a business-approved pending contract. State exactly what can be validated offline, pause credit/fulfillment promises, project backlog and deadlines, and communicate impact. | Tell customers “confirmed” when only intake succeeded. |
| “The dashboards are green, but support says orders are missing.” | Search the business status ledger and trace/correlation context, compare source IDs to ERP and fulfillment, inspect DLQ and silent filtering, and open an incident for discrepancies. | Trust broker acknowledgements or average API latency as proof of business completion. |

## 9. Failure tabletop: decide, then compare with the answer key

**Exercise:** At 09:00, the ERP write API starts timing out. Order intake continues at 150/minute. At 09:20, ERP recovers briefly and books some requests, but adapter acknowledgements time out. At 10:00, a partner sends 300 malformed orders. At 15:00, ERP reports healthy. By then, several customers cancelled pending orders, and support finds two invoices for one order. The broker dashboard shows a large but shrinking queue. Decide: (a) immediate customer status and paused actions, (b) backlog and retention check, (c) what to do with ambiguous, malformed, and cancelled work, (d) restart and replay sequence, and (e) exit criteria and incident owners.

**Answer key:**

1. Mark requests `RECEIVED_PENDING_ERP`, notify customers of delayed confirmation, and pause ERP-dependent fulfillment, invoice creation, and irreversible charges. An incident commander coordinates integration, ERP, product/support, and finance owners.
2. Estimate six-hour backlog as up to 54,000 commands under the stated sustained rate; verify actual arrivals, serialized size, retention margin, and safe catch-up rate. The 18-hour example retention covers the ideal six-hour outage plus six-hour drain with limited contingency. Throttle intake before crossing the capacity or customer promise boundary.
3. For 09:20 ambiguous calls, query ERP by stable operation ID and reconcile before retrying. Quarantine the 300 malformed orders with reason and partner contact; do not let them consume the transient retry budget. Suppress or resolve cancelled orders according to current state and policy before booking. Investigate the duplicate invoice as a financial incident even if the queue is shrinking.
4. Confirm ERP health and quota, canary verified eligible orders, then ramp within tested ERP limits while monitoring errors, p99 latency, oldest age, and discrepancies. Replay only an approved manifest of unresolved eligible IDs through an isolated lane; stop if duplicates or mismatches rise.
5. Close only when all source orders have a terminal decision or assigned exception, ERP and fulfillment records reconcile by order ID and amount, duplicate invoices are corrected with finance approval, backlog age returns to target, and support sees accurate order timelines. A green broker dashboard alone does not meet those criteria.
